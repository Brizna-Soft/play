De qué trata

Somewhere north. Listening Post K-7.
1977. You were flown in hooded. You don't know where you are, only what the post is called and what Control expects of you: find the signals, log the signals, keep the stove lit, and stay indoors.
ANOMIA is a one-room, first-person horror game about listening. You are the replacement operator of a three-metre hut on the ice, alone with a radar scope, an antenna you turn by hand, a reel-to-reel deck and a teletype that only ever speaks to you in capitals. The last operator, Varga, left before relief arrived. Varga also left tapes.
THE WORK
Every shift, Control sends a quota. Out on the scope, the contacts drift in from the dark: whistlers off the aurora, pulsars, telemetry chirps, code. Some of them sound like a choir that has forgotten the song.

• Find the blip. The radar sweep gives you a bearing.

• Turn to it. Crank the antenna until the scope locks.

• Record it. Hold steady on the reel-to-reel while the tape runs.

• Clean it. Flip the filter switches until the noise falls away and the signal comes through.

• File it. Natural, man-made or unknown. Control likes unknown. Control never asks about unknown.

It's simple. It's meant to be. The hard part is everything else.

THE HUT
The generator needs pumping. The fuses blow. The antenna ices over, and you'll see it turning white if you climb onto the bunk and look out through the slot window, out across the snow toward the mountains you can sometimes make out, toward the other hut you're sure nobody lives in. The stove needs coal. The coffee machine works, mostly.

And at night the radar shows something pale, going round and round the post, a little closer each time.

THE RULES
Nobody gives you the rules.

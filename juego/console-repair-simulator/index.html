Console Repair Simulator

Juego de PC · Casual, Indie, Simuladores · Indie, Simulación

Inherit your uncle's dusty console shop on a Tokyo back street. Sell new hardware, haggle for used consoles, then open them up on the repair mat: unscrew every screw, find the fault, swap the part, power on. Restore, grade, resell, and turn the top floor into a 90s Game Saloon.

De qué trata

Your uncle's shop. Your repair bench.
Uncle Tetsu has left you the keys to his console shop on a quiet Tokyo back street. The shelves are thin, the workshop is dusty and the top floor is taped off. You have a little cash, a toolbox and one dead handheld on the bench.

Sell new consoles and games. Buy used ones from customers who may or may not be telling you everything. Fix what is broken, one screw at a time, and turn three cramped floors into the neighbourhood's favourite retro hangout.Sell, buy and haggle
• Run the counter. Stock the shelves from delivery boxes, set prices with the price gun (it shows cost, market price and margin) and ring customers up by card or by counting out the cash change yourself.

• Buy used hardware. Customers bring in old consoles and games. Rotate the device, check its screws, run power, input and media tests, feel the weight, make sure it is complete.

• Haggle for real. Every flaw you find is leverage. Each seller has a personality, a hidden bottom line and a limited patience: the attic cleaner, the broke student, the flipper who knows exactly what it is worth. Some will not mention the fault they already know about.

• Know the risk. The counter tests only tell part of the story. Some faults only show up once the case is open on your bench.

• Show off your best work. Put restored machines in the refurbished showcase and the street window, each with a condition grade on its card.

Repair, screw by screw
• The repair mat. Sit down at the bench and the view drops to a top-down cutting mat. Rotate and zoom the device, lay the removed parts out around it and drop every screw into a magnetic tray.

• A real order of work. Every part and every screw is its own object, and nothing comes off until what holds it is gone.
